## Comparability notes and method changes

1. **Vendor change between waves.** Anaheim switched from Olinger (Baseline–W3) to Common Good (W4). Garden Grove switched from Olinger (W1) to Common Good (W2). The original project brief listed Olinger only for the Anaheim baseline. The files show Olinger templates for Anaheim Baseline, W2, and W3 and for GG W1.
2. **Question numbering changed in Anaheim W4** (for example, awareness moved from A5 to A2 and the agree statements moved from C8 to C6). Wording and scales match except one item: the "well-informed about developments" statement. In W4 it was reworded to ask specifically about OCVIBE's opening and offerings, so that series breaks at W4.
3. **Mode.** Garden Grove W2 added CATI (phone) per the deck. Earlier waves don't state their mode. The W2 data has no mode variable, so the online and phone split can't be verified.
4. **Languages.** Anaheim: English and Spanish at Baseline, English only at W4, not stated for W2/W3. Garden Grove: English, Spanish and Vietnamese at W1; English and Vietnamese at W2 (6 of 100 completes in Vietnamese).
5. **Sample size.** Anaheim n=197–269. Garden Grove n=91–100. Visit Anaheim familiarity and sentiment use smaller subgroup bases (GG W2: n=68 and n=65).
6. **Unweighted samples with shifting composition.** No wave reports weighting, so differences in sample makeup flow straight into the results. Garden Grove W1→W2 per the vendor demographics slides: Asian 51%→31%, White non-Hispanic 24%→51%, Hispanic 25%→14%, homeowners 35%→63%, married/living together 57%→79%, male 67%→73%. Anaheim Baseline→W4: homeowners 56%→43%, Boomers 19%→12%, Gen Z 7%→14%.
7. **Significance conventions differ by vendor.** Olinger W2 tested against Baseline; Olinger W3 and Common Good W4/GG W2 tested against the prior wave. Common Good's W4 Anaheim flags include several +7 to +9 point changes that a standard two-proportion test at 95% doesn't confirm (see the cumulative KPI table).
